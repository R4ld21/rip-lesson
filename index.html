EXPLAIN

What is RIP?

RIP = Routing Information Protocol.

It is an intra-domain routing protocol: it routes packets within one autonomous system.

Example: web browsing inside a school or office network.

Intra-domain ✔
EXPLAIN

Hop count

Each time a packet passes from one router to the next, that is 1 hop. Tap a router to see how far it is from Router 1.

Pick a router.

Try the limit: 8 hops

Jeepney analogy: every transfer is one hop. Maximum = 15 hops; 16 = unreachable.

EXPLAIN

How RIP works: choosing a route

RIP always picks the route with the fewest hops. Equal routes? Traffic uses both: load balancing.

Advantages and disadvantages

GENERALIZATION

To sum it up

RIP is a simple routing protocol that chooses the path with the fewest hops, sends updates every 30 seconds, and supports up to 15 hops. It is easy to set up, but it does not consider the speed or bandwidth of a route.

Reflection

A network of routers can find its way to any destination just by counting hops and trusting its neighbors. No single router needs to know the whole map, just where to send the packet next.
